Module 1: Why we cut documents up

An LLM answers from the text you put in its prompt. A 300-page policy can't go in whole: it costs tokens, may not fit, and models tend to use information in the middle of a long prompt less reliably (the "lost in the middle" effect, Liu et al. 2023). So a RAG system cuts documents into chunks, indexes them, and at question time fetches only the few chunks that matter.

Module 2: Three ways to cut

Fixed-size

Every N characters. Fast and predictable, but blind to meaning: it cuts mid-word.

Sentence-aware

Packs whole sentences up to a limit. Boundaries stay readable.

Recursive

Prefers paragraph breaks, falls back to sentences, then words. A common production default.

Hands-on: chunk your own text

Paste any text. Start with Fixed at size 60, note the mid-sentence cuts, then switch strategy. Overlap in sentence modes carries the previous sentence forward.

Beyond these: semantic chunking splits where the meaning shifts, measured by embedding similarity between neighbouring sentences. It can give cleaner topics but costs extra embedding calls, so it is not automatically better.

Module 3: Size, overlap and the retrieval test

Small chunks are precise but lose surrounding context. Large chunks keep context but dilute meaning, add noise and cost more tokens. Overlap (commonly 10 to 20% of chunk size) protects facts that sit on a boundary, at the price of duplicated storage. English text averages roughly 4 characters per token, which the lab uses as an estimate.

Challenge (+40): make retrieval work

Question: How many days do I have to request a refund? Tune the settings until the top-ranked chunk contains "30 days", ends on a clean sentence boundary, and the chunk size is 160 characters or less (no cheating with giant chunks).

Module 4: From lab to real pipeline

Load›Clean›Chunk›Embed›Store›Retrieve›Generate

Chunking sits in the middle, so every earlier mistake (OCR noise, repeated page headers) and every later step (embedding quality, retrieval) depends on it. Store metadata with each chunk: source file, page or section, timestamp.

Beginner mistakes: zero overlap, one setting for every document type, flattening tables so rows lose their headers, dropping metadata, and never measuring retrieval on real questions.
